# Rail operations — native v0.21.1

Rail freight now has two stages: a supplier locomotive brings the train to the original receiving siding, then your owned shunter moves its cars around the factory. The supplier can leave while the cars remain. After unloading, assemble the empty cars on that siding and request a locomotive to collect them. These controls are in the native game; the hosted browser remains v0.18.0.

Start with the track layout, before ordering a train. In **Railway**, click **+ Yard access switch**. Its default start is E80, S5. It replaces 20 meters of the original siding with a diverging turnout made from seven panels and opens a factory branch at E100, S10. Extend that branch with Straight rail, 90° curve, Diverging switch and Converging switch. The normal construction crew handles and installs the new panels; Creative completes them immediately. The replacement of the inherited siding is currently schematic: its old steel is not recovered into stock.

Also click **Build siding exit**. This plans a separate seven-panel connection from the east end of the receiving siding back to the main line. Finish it and remove any blocking buffer before releasing the supplier engine. Keep the mainline route clear. In normal play, order the required materials and assign construction equipment and workers; Creative installs the exit immediately. The factory access switch and the east exit perform different jobs, so you need both.

Use **+ Receiving point** to name a clear interval on `BOOTSTRAP-SIDING`. With the default E80 access switch, the suggested offset is 30 meters from the siding's western start: center E55, with 40 meters of usable length. This fits a one-car train and avoids the replacement switch at E80–E100. Adjust the interval for your train, keeping it on clear receiving track. The Purchase / hire preview reports the complete train length, including its locomotive; all of it must fit before the train can arrive.

In **Purchase / hire**, choose Rail, enter the materials for the batch, choose the receiving point and an unloading stockyard, then place the order. A batch can contain several flatcars, each with its own ID and manifest. Supplier reception remains on the original siding. Named locations on the rest of the connected factory tracks are destinations for the owned shunter.

When the train stops, open its delivery in **Deliveries** or click a car in **Railway** and open its delivery controls. Click **Release supplier locomotive**. The cars are secured where they stand; the engine uncouples and leaves through the east exit. Watch its state and wait until it is gone before shunting. This stops the supplier engine's idling charges while your freight remains available on site.

Click **Railway → + Shunter** to buy an owned diesel locomotive. It costs $68,000 plus $240 for rail delivery. Keep the receiving route and its parking position clear. Assign an equipment operator as its driver, either when ordering or in the locomotive inspector. The worker must be free and on duty. The driver walks to the locomotive, boards, and walks to turnout levers when a route needs changing. The inspector shows its fuel, current assignment and any waiting reason.

Select an installed rail panel and use **Designate named rail location** to mark a factory dock or parking point. Give the point enough usable track length for the intended cars. Its interval can span connected panels. Naming a location does not build missing track or clear a buffer stop. A runaround or parallel unloading layout needs real switches and connections at both ends where the locomotive must reach the other side of a train.

To move freight, open the delivery inspector and check a contiguous block of cars from an exposed end of that train. Choose the shunter, choose the named destination, and click **Shunt selected cars**. The service chooses a clear approach and a pulling or pushing arrangement. The driver couples, moves the cars over the connected rails, and uncouples at the destination. Keep clearance for the locomotive as well as the cars. The car register shows each car's current location, remaining material and linked stockyard.

To unload, choose and apply a physical stockyard in that delivery inspector, check the cars you want unloaded, and click **Start unloading**. Your equipment, operators and any required ground helper handle the real cargo. A full or inaccessible yard produces a waiting reason. You may change the stockyard between lifts. Before moving cars again, click **Pause unloading after current lift**; the current handling operation finishes safely and no new lift begins.

For returns, use the shunter to bring the empty cars together on the original siding. Moving them to the same named return point lets the service place them next to cars already assembled there. The final consist must be contiguous and empty. Then use the shunter inspector's **Drive shunter to named location** to park it clear of the pickup and departure route.

Click **Railway → Collect empty cars**, check the empty deliveries you want returned together, choose the collection point if desired, and click **Request mainline locomotive**. A supplier engine approaches the assembled cars, couples and tests the brakes, then takes the empty train out onto the main line. Follow its phase in the **Empty return trains** register in Deliveries. Cars retain their identities and histories when they leave. Loaded cars cannot be returned by this control.

Only one rail movement runs at a time. This release handles flatcar goods, selected-car shunting and empty returns; it does not include tanker operation, direct manual shunter driving or an engine shed. Supplier coupling, uncoupling and brake work are timed schematic service-crew steps. To refuel a shunter, park within 8 meters of a diesel barrel, assign a driver and click **Refuel stopped shunter**. Diesel is deducted from that barrel and added immediately to the tank; physical fuel carrying and pumping for locomotives are further work.

If a movement waits, open its delivery or shunter inspector and inspect the linked obstruction. Clear the track, destination, buffer stop or another vehicle before retrying. A named point alone cannot bypass physical clearance. Save files retain cars, manifests, locomotives, assignments and movements, so you can stop and resume a yard without losing its railway records. The same walkthrough is available as simple paragraphs through **Railway → Rail management help**.


## Editing rails and buffer stops

Open **Railway** and select a completed panel in **Installed track**. Its inspector distinguishes the selected panel from the remaining panels in its curve or turnout assembly. Choose **Review this panel recovery…** or **Review whole curve/turnout…**. The review lists the actual material types, quantities, weights and identities, including attached stops. It shows automatic stockyard destinations and any blocking constraint before you submit the edit. Preview destinations are not reservations; normal work rechecks capacity and access when handling the material.

In normal play, **Plan physical recovery** creates work for your crew and lifting equipment. A worker unfastens and rigs the material, the machine lifts and carries it to storage, and attached stops are recovered before their supporting rail. Open the linked work order to assign equipment and follow progress. Trains, reserved movements, named locations and conflicting work can prevent recovery. Cancel unbuilt plans through **Work**; recover completed infrastructure through its inspector.

Use **Railway → Buffer stops / editing** for the installed, available and incoming stop counts, a focused purchase form, and installation at real completed open endpoints. Select an installed stop in Railway or the yard and choose **Review buffer recovery…**. Stops occupy a finite 2 × 2 meter stockyard slot and keep their identities when recovered and reinstalled. Purchases bring material to the site; installation is separate physical work. The review explains missing stock, workers, equipment or storage. The Railway register links stop stock, incoming orders and active stop work.

Creative uses the same review but offers **Install instantly** or **Recover instantly**. Recovery is immediate and still requires finite stockyard capacity; an impossible edit changes nothing. Installing a stop reuses available recovered stock before supplying a new Creative asset. Track extensions move their incoming stop to an open end. Connecting track, including either turnout tail, recovers redundant stops into storage; ordinary construction performs that recovery physically. Secured stops continue to block train routes until removed.

### Buffer-stop representation decision (#40)

A stop is mounted rail infrastructure with its own stable identity and a shared placement/inspection/recovery workflow. It does not contribute extra track length. We evaluated treating it as a five-meter straight panel with a stop in its middle. That would add or replace steel, shift the stopping plane by half a panel, change usable dock lengths, and complicate recovery of existing track and active connected construction. Keeping the mount and supporting steel as separate accounted assets preserves those physical meanings. Panel recovery includes its attached stops, and the common editing review presents both together. This resolves the lifecycle inconsistency without silently changing track geometry or allowing trains through secured stops.
