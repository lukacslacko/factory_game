# Work and operations

A work order is a planning relationship above actual physical tasks. Office installation contains a foundation subgroup and assembly task; the foundation contains slab tasks. A paving drag creates one area order, and adjacent siding panels share a rail stretch. The tasks remain independent serialized state machines with their own reservations, cargo, workers, material movements, and costs.

Equipment assignment follows the nearest explicit choice on a task or its ancestors. A child override takes precedence. The newest explicit request for a machine takes priority after current physical work or freight batch finishes safely. An assigned machine is reserved for those descendants; it cannot drift into unrelated automatic jobs or delivery unloading. Clearing an assignment restores parent inheritance or the shared automatic pool. An assignment never makes a forklift lift an office or lay rail, invents an operator, skips fueling, or bypasses an obstructed approach.

The Work table opens with active work. Expand controls show the hierarchy; sorting compares siblings and retains descendant blocks. Work ID, worker, operator, current equipment, preferred equipment, stock, and parent references lead to inspectors. Historical stock and movement references expose their retained history even after consumption. Text and column filters narrow every register; sorting/filtering happens before pagination.

Parking uses a grid coordinate at the machine center and one of four cardinal directions. The visible bay belongs to equipment and can be changed using a yard click or inspector coordinates. The full chassis must fit, including between a shed's physical posts and back wall. An idle machine waits for a real operator, boarding and routing; it does not move unoccupied. Fuel is consumed while driving. Missing operators, fuel, and routes retain visible parking reasons.

Worker schedules use the displayed clock. Hours can cross midnight. Always on is the default for new and existing workers. Shift end gates new assignments, while a current operation safely completes. Workers then park their equipment, exit the cab, walk to the bus, and board. Their same identities return for their next shift. Each charter bus uses the road and passenger transitions, and its $180 charge is recorded. The bus can wait for a delayed passenger rather than leaving them behind. The game does not progress while closed.

## Batch procurement

In **Purchase**, set a row quantity and click **Add** to collect items in the order batch. Workers of different roles can share one bus; material types can share one freight load. Change **Material transport** to preview road or rail loads, then use **Place batch order**. **Hire** and **Order** remain immediate single-row purchases. Clearing or removing draft lines creates no order or cost.

The preview shows cargo weight, planned carrier count by type, and total committed cost. Unit and line weights update with the selected quantity. Weights use kg and metric tons (t). Workers use seats rather than cargo mass; service rows do not claim a material weight. Road freight is limited to 12 t and a 6 m deck, rail freight to 48 t and a 16 m deck, and buses to 12 passengers. Deck packing accounts for each material's physical stack limit, so more carriers can be required before the weight limit. Equipment and services retain dedicated road vehicles.

Each packed load has an independent order, transport charge, arrival, and invoice. A mixed delivery inspector lists every line and its received count; the cargo does not collapse into the first item type. Unloading retains actual site equipment, operators, riggers, stack capacity, and access checks. Save/reload preserves each line's progress, including a load already being handled. Single-item orders from older saves retain their contents.

SQL `orders.mass_kg` reports the carrier's full ordered mass. `order_lines` exposes `order_id`, `line`, `item`, `qty`, `arrived`, and `mass_kg` for analysis; `mass_kg` is the full ordered line mass, not its remaining cargo mass. An example is `SELECT order_id, item, qty, arrived, mass_kg FROM order_lines ORDER BY order_id, line`.

## Diagnostic bundles

The recorder is passive and separate from the simulation save. It retains changed job/delivery states, events, material movements, UI commands, and actor positions every 0.2 simulation seconds while moving. Each actor sample includes direction, velocity, path/destination, blocker, assignment, cargo, and equipment working reach. Up to 18,000 entries or 6 MB of entry text are retained, with four recent full checkpoints. Export includes the current yard; rolled-off entry count is explicit. Local IndexedDB is written with normal autosaves. It is never automatically uploaded.

`tools/review-recording.mjs` accepts an exported bundle and reports incomplete work, waiting deliveries, and prolonged low-displacement movement windows, including heading reversals and blocking IDs. A traffic wait is not automatically a bug. Compare the route and load envelope with its neighbors. Full checkpoint/current states can be extracted into ordinary save files for exact state inspection, but the trace is not a guaranteed deterministic input replay. Diagnostic exports should not be passed directly to the save importer.

## Physical limits

Routing uses sampled swept compound shapes, not a rigid-body engine. Open sheds have solid post bases and a back wall; solid buildings, light bases, cabinets, corridor poles, stored loads, and a resting buffer affect both paths and movement execution. Carrying a buffer removes its resting obstacle while the lifting envelope remains. Approaches validate actual machine poses and turning space. Legacy paths are checked and replanned during execution rather than allowed to cross new obstacles.

The operating model still uses fixed supplier berths and road routes, straight buildable track, simplified fastening/rigging, and local charter buses. A transit route editor, train dispatch, precise articulated joint controls, maintenance, weather, production, and worker welfare remain future work.

## Overlapping physical work

A slab job retains its finishing worker and supported slab after safe machine withdrawal and tool stow. The machine and operator are released at that point. Even with one builder, a following slab task can reserve its source and drive to its pickup approach while that builder levels the previous slab. It cannot rig or lift without an eligible worker; the pending task states that requirement. Finishing and prefetch remain separate saved operations, and canceling either releases only its own resources. Idle automatic workers in a future placement or withdrawal area receive a real walking route clear during pickup preparation; manually controlled workers still require the player to move them. Executed machine movement continues to check every pedestrian. If an idle, empty machine blocks a planned dock, its seated automatic operator can receive a real clearing trip; a manual operator remains under player control. Crew escape paths can go around a stock row and the full turning envelope rather than requiring a sideways step.

Receiving dispatch reserves an assisting rigger and starts their walk while the operator boards and drives to the carrier. The last parcel must actually lift clear of the deck before transport is released. A carrier can drive out while the site machine continues to storage. The delivery remains active until both transport and placement are finished; its inspector explicitly distinguishes a carrier that has left from continuing site handling. Material counts represent unloaded cargo as well as stored goods.

At 1× the calendar, physical movement, work, wages, and attendance all use real seconds during active foreground play. 3× and 10× multiply the same seconds. Pause and closing the game do not accrue time. Supplier lead time starts at three minutes at 1×. Existing saved calendar dates and past ledger entries retain their original values.

Actual collision-checked equipment travel compacts dirt under wheel/track strips. Repeated passes gradually darken those lanes, suppress grass, and persist in saves. Surface-aware routing modestly prefers concrete and permanent hardstanding, then established dirt, then ordinary dirt; long detours and extra turns can still outweigh the preference. Short precise loading approaches retain their deliberate geometry. Wear saturates per cell and the map retains at most 12,000 cells. New cells stop accumulating at that bound without deleting established paths. This feature does not yet implement component damage or seasonal rutting.

## Shed assembly and material ownership

A delivered shed kit remains cargo until it is lowered and unpacked at the construction site. It then becomes one kit in assembly. The builder anchors bases, rigs each member, and walks to its fastening position; the equipment operator collects, raises and positions each member. Posts precede roof frames, roof sections, wall panels and bracing. An excavator erects the shed; a forklift can unload its packed kit. The builder sets up a supplied assembly ladder, climbs to elevated fastening points, works, and descends before leaving the component. Already erected members remain visible and obstruct workers and equipment as appropriate. Completed shed geometry uses the same component poses. Canceling a partial build reverses the work and returns one whole kit; it does not discard already unpacked material.

Automatic work allows any combination of Receiving deliveries, Paving, Building, Rail work and Recovery. All and None are convenience presets. Changes preserve current loaded work and take effect for new assignments; explicit job assignments retain their priority and operator/fuel/access constraints.

## One automatic machine per work order

The outermost work group owns its automatically selected machine across paving cells and foundation subgroups. Independent groups remain parallel. Machine ownership is distinct from material reservations and explicit equipment assignments. After a slab is safely placed and the equipment is released, its worker can continue finishing while the same machine fetches the next slab. A machine may receive missing material without inviting a second machine into its paving group. Role, qualification, fuel, operator availability or explicit reservations permit a safe future handoff; an active physical job retains its real crew and load.

A material carrier retains its automatic receiving machine between lifts. Another carrier cannot take that machine while freight remains, and automatic parking waits between receiving lifts. Explicit construction priorities or direct unloading can override the future reservation after current cargo is supported. The final parcel still frees the carrier immediately after lifting clear. Ownership never adds an extra machine, worker, fuel or stock.

## Curves and turnouts

Rail planning begins at an exposed, tangent-matched endpoint. Use Rail end to find the original siding end, select a layout and bend, rotate with R, and click the joint. The preview and cost/weight bill describe the actual pieces. Curves require six curved panels; turnouts require one points assembly, three regular straight panels, and one each of frog, closure and exit panels. Buy missing uses their real stock and incoming quantities. Jobs retain one parent equipment assignment while each panel undergoes physical rigging, staging, buffer handling, laying and fastening.

A chained plan can be drawn before upstream construction finishes, but its first job waits for an installed, completed connecting endpoint. Queue priority cannot bypass earlier physical panels. A canceled work inspector offers Resume; it preserves completed track and staged stock and rejects a now-obstructed bed without partially resuming the group. New assembly recovery waits for the next railway checkpoint. The original straight-panel recovery still works.

The Railway register lists actual installed topology and exposes uncapped ends. Completed turnouts have a manual Straight/Branch lever in the points inspector. A request queues a worker on foot; they walk to the handle and throw it over four seconds. The old selected route remains committed until completion. Saving preserves the operation, and cancellation restores the old setting safely. No train is yet dispatched over these player-built paths. Supplier trains keep their original route; protection of the extra branch, shunter operation, a driver and engine shed require the next approved checkpoint.

## Rail work groups and dedicated helpers

Connected pieces within the same rail work order share the physical BUFFER-001. It is unbolted/lifted once, remains a rendered obstacle on clear side ground during intermediate panels, and is lifted/fastened at the last endpoint. A canceled continuation returns it to the latest installed endpoint; a between-panel cancellation queues physical cleanup instead of snapping it back. Turnout branch panels cannot claim the through-line buffer.

The parent rail work inspector accepts a staging machine and a distinct installation excavator. Original panel job records perform both phases: staging does not add material demand or duplicate installed panels. Each machine retains its actual operator and support worker. One panel ahead may be carried/staged while installation continues; a job-owned supported stack is unavailable to other receiving work. Machine withdrawal precedes ownership transfer. Explicit crew assignments reserve both machines from unrelated automatic work. The staging machine can receive its own missing rail panels first, and the installer performs paving recovery required by its track bed. Clearing the crew finishes active handling before normal dispatch resumes.

Support-worker assignment is one builder/engineer per equipment. Helpers are reserved from other machines' automatic rigging and construction; an unavailable assigned helper produces a waiting reason rather than borrowing someone else. Idle helpers walk to clear nearby ground. Manual movement, rest, commute, shift end, and active physical tasks retain control. An operator remains separately necessary.
