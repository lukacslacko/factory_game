# Ground cover and traveled paths

The starter site is a humid European industrial edge, with dense, stable, varied ground cover. Short grass is widespread; taller weeds and small, medium, and larger bushes appear in deterministic patches. The soil beneath is a consistent damp earth color, with only fine close-range relief. Green variation comes from actual instanced plant geometry rather than broad mottled colors painted onto the ground. Roads, installed track, paving, buildings, and stored material suppress ground plants within their footprints. Plants are never randomly repositioned during play or after loading a save.

Accepted machine movement records two related facts per one-meter dirt cell: wheel or track compaction, and the most recent chassis passage. A passing machine clears bushes and tall weeds along its swept width. Grass remains after one pass and thins as repeated passes deepen the ruts. The filtered ground material shows those established paths; automatic routing modestly prefers them, while concrete remains preferable. A parked or transported machine does not mark the ground.

Unused ruts fade over six simulated hours. Grass returns with the fading wear, weeds begin returning after 30 quiet minutes and mature over the next two and a half hours, and bushes begin returning after six quiet hours and reach full size after eighteen. Repeated movement restarts this recovery. The save records both wear intensity and last disturbance time, each capped at 12,000 cells; mature, unworn history is discarded. This is an ecological presentation layer, not a supply or clearing job: no worker or invoice is created when a plant grows or is brushed aside.

The native renderer uses the same saved simulation records as routing. Its filtered soil wear texture is updated at most twice per real second, and foliage meshes retain their original transforms. A spatial index limits vegetation updates to plants near cells whose paving, stock, vehicle occupancy, wear, or disturbance changed; a quiet snapshot does not rescan the whole meadow. The browser prototype continues to show the established dirt marks but does not reproduce the full native growth staging.
